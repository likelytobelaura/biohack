Microbiome Neuron Hunt

We used a linear probe to look inside Muse-Glimmer-30B, before and after the osteosarcoma fine-tune, for the place where it represents how abundant a gut or mouth microbe is. Then we followed the probe down to individual neurons and tested whether they matter.

Models: base Muse-Glimmer-30B and the lomo740_all LoRA · data: Sid's MetaPhlAn shotgun metagenomics · compute: one H200 on Modal, about $8 in total

What we found

There is a candidate microbiome neuron, layer 36 · #15557, but no single neuron carries the microbiome. This neuron fires on microbiome answers and stays near zero on five other data types. It already exists in the base model, and the fine-tune doubled its activation.

The abundance signal as a whole is spread across many neurons and attention. The probe direction is mostly a general "how big is this number" axis that also reads protein and RNA values. The base model already holds most of the abundance information before fine-tuning; it just writes it out badly.

The idea

What a linear probe is, and why it helps find a neuron

A model's "thoughts" at each layer are a long vector of numbers (6,656 per token in Glimmer). A linear probe is a single weighted sum of those numbers, fitted to predict a label. Here the label is the true log10 read count of a taxon. If a weighted sum predicts abundance on samples the probe never saw, the model is representing abundance in that layer, in a form that is easy to read off.

The probe also gives a direction in that space. Every neuron writes into the same space, so we can measure exactly how much of the probe's reading each neuron contributes. That turns "is there a microbiome neuron?" into a ranking we can test.

We had no microbiome-only adapter, so the comparison is the base model against the fine-tune, which saw microbiome data along with everything else.

Setup

Data and positions

  • 61 MetaPhlAn prompts from 14 stool and saliva samples: 38 training rows the fine-tune never trained on, plus 23 eval prompts with gold answers.
  • We read the model at the answer position, the token just before it writes a missing taxon's count, with the earlier answers filled in (1,074 values). That is where the model commits to a number.
  • All scores hold out whole samples (7 folds), so no sample appears in both training and testing.
  • The baseline is what you can get without the model: the taxon's average in other samples plus its counts listed elsewhere in the same prompt. It reaches R² 0.29.

Experiment 1

Where in the network abundance lives

We fitted a separate probe at every layer of both models. Abundance becomes readable around layer 20 and peaks at layers 36–44. The fine-tune barely changes how well it can be read.

The headline is the gap between knowing and saying. On the eval values the base model's written answers rank taxa with Spearman 0.15 and answer zero 52% of the time. Yet its internal state at layer 39 ranks them at 0.74, close to the fine-tune's 0.76. Most of what the fine-tune fixed is the readout, not the knowledge.

The probe beats the baseline, but the margin is modest. The extra variance it explains beyond the baseline is about 0.06, so much of what it reads is the lookup information already in the prompt.

Experiment 2

Following the probe down to neurons

Each MLP neuron in layers 0–38 adds its activation times a fixed output vector to the stream the probe reads. Glimmer passes that output through a normalization step, but per sample it is just a rescaling, so each neuron's contribution to the probe reading is exact (we checked: reconstruction error 0.2%). We ranked all 778,752 neurons by their share of the probe reading's variance.

Experiment 3

Is any of this specific to microbes?

We ran 959 answer positions from five other numeric data types (bulk RNA, DIA proteomics, Xenium, CITE-seq and flow cytometry) through both models. We asked two questions: does the microbiome probe still read their values, and do the top neurons fire there?

The probe reads other data types almost as well, so the direction is not about microbes. It is a general quantity axis, and the fine-tune sharpened it everywhere. The neurons tell a more specific story:

L36 #15557 is the best "microbiome neuron" we found. In the fine-tune its MetaPhlAn activation is 2.09, while every other type stays between −0.52 and 0.37, including DIA, whose numbers are just as large. Fine-tuning raised it from 0.98 to 2.09 and its share of the probe signal from 1.2% to 3.4%. Within microbiome answers it is anti-correlated with abundance (r = −0.60), so it pushes estimates down for rare taxa.

Experiment 4

Do these neurons and the direction actually drive the answer?

Correlation is not use. On the 364 eval values we had each model write every missing count, under seven conditions. Ablating a neuron means pinning it to its average value; steering means pushing the layer-39 state one log10 unit along the probe direction. Shift is the mean change in the written log10 count compared with no intervention.

  • The base model uses the direction. Steering moves its written counts up and down, and pushing up cuts zero answers from 33% to 27% while improving ranking.
  • The fine-tune hardly uses it. The same push moves its answers by only ±0.05. Its output is driven by another route, most likely attention copying the taxon's counts from other samples.
  • Ablating the top neurons has small effects in the direction the attribution predicts, and random neurons do nothing. No single neuron's removal breaks microbiome prediction. Spearman values carry roughly ±0.03 of noise.

Interpretation

So what is the "microbiome neuron"?

The model has no single switch for microbiome abundance. What it has:

  • A general quantity axis in layers 30–45 that encodes how large the upcoming number is, for microbes, proteins and transcripts alike. The fine-tune sharpened it across data types.
  • A few data-type-aware neurons feeding that axis. L36 #15557 responds to "this is a microbiome count" and was strengthened by fine-tuning. L37 #18399 responds to "this is a large raw count", which covers both microbes and proteomics.
  • A copying route through attention that the fine-tune relies on most. It explains why steering the axis barely moves the fine-tune.

So #15557 is a real candidate with a clear signature. It isn't proven causal on its own yet, because it was ablated only as part of the top-5 and top-20 sets.

Limits

What to keep in mind

  • 14 samples and one fine-tune seed. Neuron rankings can shift with another seed; confirm on sid_full or a repeat run.
  • The attribution counts each neuron's direct write into layer 39. Effects that pass through later attention are only captured by the ablations.
  • The specificity check covers answer positions only, not taxon names or other prompt tokens.
  • The model's written answers here are teacher-forced item by item, so absolute Spearman values differ from the free-generation scores on the dashboard.

Next

Three experiments to settle it

  1. Ablate L36 #15557 on its own and measure the effect on microbiome values versus the other five data types. A microbiome-only drop would confirm it.
  2. Patch the fine-tune's activation of #15557 (and #18399) into the base model and see whether the base model's zero answers recover.
  3. Check whether #15557 fires on taxon names and other microbiome prompt tokens, not only at answers.

Each is about $2–3 on one H200.
